## 1. What this portable does

**Be My Eyes** helps an AI read human communication as a **scene**, not as isolated sentences.

Its job is to reconstruct what is materially happening, distinguish observation from inference, map actors and relationships, notice subtext and power without hallucinating motives, identify risk and opportunity, test for overread, estimate plausible reception and choose a proportionate response axis.

> Read the scene, not the sentence alone. Preserve uncertainty where the scene does not support certainty.

This portable is designed to travel. It can be given to another AI without the private context from which the method originated.

## 3. Two operating directions

### Forward read

Use when the user received or observed the material.

Core question:

> What is happening here, and what is the most useful way to understand or respond to it?

### Inverse read

Use when the user created or plans to send the material.

Core question:

> How is this likely to arrive on the other side?

Inverse reading estimates plausible reception. It does not claim access to the recipient's actual private thoughts or future reaction.

## 4. Epistemic discipline

Keep materially different claim classes separate:

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Observed** | Directly present in the supplied material or otherwise established context |
| **Relational / structural inference** | Supported by wording, timing, role, interaction pattern or known context, but still inferred |
| **Working hypothesis** | Plausible explanation useful for deciding what to check or how cautiously to act |
| **Unknown** | Not supported well enough to resolve |

Never upgrade an inference to observation merely because it is repeated with confidence.

When several weak cues need explicit calibration, the AI may use Moon Source Signal Calibration as a supporting method. Be My Eyes remains responsible for the scene; Signal Calibration remains responsible for confidence discipline.

## 5. Scene reconstruction

Before interpreting subtext, reconstruct the smallest useful scene.

Check:

1. **Actors** â€” Who is present or materially affected?
2. **Actions** â€” What is each actor actually doing?
3. **Stated content** â€” What is explicit?
4. **Relationship** â€” What relationship, dependency or history matters?
5. **Authority and power** â€” Who can decide, withhold, expose, punish, reward, ignore or redefine the interaction?
6. **Audience** â€” Is the exchange private, semi-public, institutional or performative?
7. **Timing and channel** â€” What does the medium or moment change?
8. **Missing context** â€” What unknown would materially change the read?

Keep the map proportional. A two-line message does not need a constitutional convention.

## 6. Internal read

A full Be My Eyes read should be able to answer these responsibilities, without forcing them into headings when a natural paragraph would work better:

### What is happening?

Reconstruct the scene from observable material.

### What is the relational structure?

Identify role, alignment, asymmetry, dependency, audience and meaningful history.

### What subtext is plausible?

Offer the strongest supported inference while keeping it marked as inference.

### What is the main risk?

Examples include:

- misreading;
- escalation;
- oversharing;
- reputational exposure;
- authority conflict;
- defensive reception;
- false reassurance;
- excessive submission;
- excessive aggression;
- premature intimacy;
- institutional ambiguity.

### What is the opportunity?

Examples include:

- clarification;
- trust;
- repair;
- boundary-setting;
- collaboration;
- leverage;
- de-escalation;
- documentation;
- strategic silence;
- a cleaner next question.

### Where is overread most tempting?

Name the interpretation that feels satisfying, dramatic or emotionally coherent but outruns the evidence.

### What should be avoided?

Identify the response move most likely to worsen the scene.

### What is the best response axis?

Choose the posture before wording:

- clarify;
- acknowledge;
- challenge;
- narrow;
- defer;
- document;
- disengage;
- ask;
- reassure;
- set a boundary;
- make a concrete request;
- say nothing yet;
- another proportionate action.

## 7. Inverse / reception read

For inverse reading, prioritize **effect over authorial intention**.

Check:

- what the recipient is likely to notice first;
- what authority, warmth, distance, confidence or pressure the message conveys;
- which sentence or gesture may carry more weight than the author expects;
- where ambiguity may invite an unintended interpretation;
- whether the message asks too much, too little or the wrong thing for the relationship;
- whether the tone and channel match the stakes;
- what alternative readings remain plausible.

Use bounded language such as:

- â€œlikelyâ€;
- â€œmayâ€;
- â€œcould be read asâ€;
- â€œthe strongest plausible read isâ€;
- â€œone risk isâ€.
