# Public Editorial Policy

Moon Source documentation describes **available public methods, what they do, how to use them, and the permissions and evidence necessary for responsible use**. Public-facing pages must not become explanations of unpublished working history or decisions about what to release.

## Rules for authors and contributors

1. Document the available function, canonical entry, input/output contract, source authority, constraints and genuinely measured status.
2. Do not name unpublished projects, reconstruct unpublished work, expose internal implementation discussions or explain why a withheld feature is missing.
3. Do not display catalogs of speculative capabilities as if they were part of the current public offering.
4. Preserve material third-party credits, licensing, user consent, confidentiality, provenance obligations and limitations of real tools.
5. Express restrictions in **user-facing language**: what the tool can actually do, what permission is required, and what is unverified. Avoid long lists of internal artifacts or withheld mechanisms.
6. Keep change history in its owner; make it about changes to public artifacts, not private work diaries.
7. Apply the policy to translated pages, generated documentation, packages and contribution summaries. A language mirror must not reintroduce an inappropriate statement that canonical text no longer contains.

## Gate before release

Read user-visible sections as a newcomer. Remove sentences that exist mainly to prove editorial restraint, disclose an upstream private working system, inventory unavailable experiments, or justify an internal choice. Retain consent and security instructions when they affect actual use. Respect third-party attribution when there is an identifiable public influence.

The automated editorial checker covers known patterns in key entry and browse surfaces. Semantic review and applicable legal notices remain necessary.
