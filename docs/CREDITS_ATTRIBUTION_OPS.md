# 🧾 Credits & Attribution Ops — Moon Source component

## Intellectual lineage, content custody & immaterial-asset protection

- **distributed with:** Moon Source
- **canonical repository:** https://github.com/luahelenammc/Moon-Source
- **canonical path:** `docs/CREDITS_ATTRIBUTION_OPS.md`
- **project-specific use authority:** [Moon Source Use & Attribution](../MOON_SOURCE_USE_AND_ATTRIBUTION.md)
- **human creator and final authority of Moon Source:** Lua Helena Moon Martins Cardoso (Moon)
- **AI-assisted coauthorial development:** Moon + Áurion
- **status:** active reusable public component

**MSL profile:** 5.1-compatible Markdown-native method surface; preserve identity, custody and permission boundaries.

Credits & Attribution Ops is a reusable meta-operational component for protecting the continuity of intellectual and other immaterial material as it is created, shared, transformed, generated, mirrored, combined, published, superseded or archived.

Its purpose is broader than producing credits. It keeps enough of a material's **identity, origin, authorship, lineage, custody, transformation history, permission envelope, disclosure boundary, derivative relationships, attribution inheritance and evidence** recoverable that the material does not lose who or what it is merely because it moved or changed.

In this component, **protection** means operational and evidentiary protection. It does not create legal rights, resolve ownership disputes, grant permission or provide enforcement. It creates a disciplined chain of custody for immaterial material so that legal, ethical, editorial, research, governance or publication decisions can be made from a more legible record.

The central question is not only:

> Who should receive credit?

It is:

> What is this material, where did it come from, who contributed what, what happened to it, which version is canonical, what use basis is actually known, what may travel, what descendants exist, and what must remain attached to the lineage as it changes?

This module is intentionally universal. It can operate inside Moon Source or be transplanted into another source-governed project without making that project subordinate to Moon Source.

It does not decide how Moon Source itself may be used. Legal reuse permission for Moon Source files comes from the applicable standard license routed by [Moon Source Licensing](../LICENSING.md); project-specific plain-language use framing lives in [Moon Source Use & Attribution](../MOON_SOURCE_USE_AND_ATTRIBUTION.md). This protocol preserves provenance and custody but cannot invent permission for third-party material.

## 1. What this component protects

Credits & Attribution Ops can be applied to any immaterial material whose identity or lineage matters, including:

- methods, frameworks and conceptual models;
- prompts, context architectures and procedures;
- code, algorithms and technical designs;
- taxonomies, schemas and structural grammars;
- research notes, documents and analyses;
- datasets, corpora and curated collections;
- agents, personas and reusable behavioral specifications;
- images, media, visual assets and design systems;
- generated outputs and human-AI composite outputs;
- translations, summaries, excerpts, remixes and adaptations;
- forks, mirrors, snapshots and archives;
- tacit knowledge once it becomes materially expressed.

Not every influence deserves a ledger. The operation should be proportional to the value, risk, ambiguity and expected circulation of the material.

## 2. Why attribution is only one output

Attribution is the visible credit surface of a larger protection system.

A usable intellectual chain of custody must be able to preserve more than a name. It may need to preserve:

- which artifact or idea is being discussed;
- the earliest recoverable source and upstream lineage;
- who authored, adapted, maintained or transmitted which part;
- the relationship between original, derivative, mirror and composite forms;
- what changed during transformation;
- which version or path is canonical;
- what permission or usage basis is actually known;
- which material must remain private or bounded;
- which descendants inherit lineage or attribution obligations;
- which evidence supports the record.

A credit line can be generated from that record. The record cannot be reconstructed reliably from a credit line alone.

## 3. Failure modes this component is designed to resist

| Failure mode | What goes wrong |
|---|---|
| **Source laundering** | Material passes through people, models or projects until its upstream origin disappears. |
| **Authorship collapse** | Human, donor, adapter, maintainer and AI-assisted contributions are flattened into one vague authorship claim. |
| **Lineage amnesia** | A current artifact loses the ancestor needed to understand where it came from. |
| **Silent appropriation** | Absorbed material is presented as wholly local without recording the material upstream contribution. |
| **Permission drift** | “Can inspect” gradually becomes “can copy, modify or redistribute” without evidence for the expansion. |
| **Derivative ambiguity** | A copy, translation, adaptation, fork, mirror or remix is presented without identifying what relationship it has to the source. |
| **Canonical identity loss** | Multiple copies exist and nobody can recover which path, version or artifact governs the lineage. |
| **Credit dilution** | Attribution becomes weaker at each transport step until it vanishes. |
| **AI laundering** | Material enters an AI-mediated workflow and the generated result is treated as provenance-free merely because a model intervened. |
| **AI-mediated lineage drift** | A model recomposes donor concepts, terms, schemas or responsibility patterns into locally coherent work faster than provenance travels with them, making external lineage appear native. |
| **Attribution totalization** | A real influence signal is inflated into an unsupported claim of total derivation, parenthood, ownership, percentage ancestry or exclusive origin. |
| **Topology-lineage collapse** | A current integration, dependency or downstream relationship is mistaken for historical architectural lineage, or vice versa. |
| **Composite opacity** | Several material sources contribute to one output but the synthesis is presented as if it had a single origin. |
| **Disclosure-boundary erosion** | A public credit, mirror or derivative leaks private lineage, confidential material or protected implementation detail. |

The module does not guarantee that these failures cannot occur. It creates the records and checks needed to make them harder to occur silently.

## 4. Governing laws

- Absorbing a resource does not erase its origin.
- Transformation can create new local authorship without erasing material upstream lineage.
- AI mediation is not a provenance reset when identifiable source material materially conditions an output.
- Local implementation authorship and upstream architectural lineage can coexist.
- A lineage correction does not by itself create coauthorship, ownership, parenthood or control.
- Current topology, integration direction or dependency does not by itself establish historical lineage.
- Resemblance alone is weak evidence; explicit donor/adaptation records, dated chronology and mechanism-level correspondence are stronger.
- Access, visibility, discussion or availability do not create authority or permission.
- Attribution does not create permission, ownership, endorsement, partnership, validation or adoption.
- Custody is not automatically ownership; possession of a copy is not semantic authority.
- A mirror does not become canonical because it is easier to access.
- A fingerprint can support exact artifact identity; it does not prove authorship, legality or permission.
- A provenance record does not make the recorded material canonical.
- Unknown permission, origin, authorship or relationship should remain unknown rather than being filled by confident fiction.
- Material influence should be preserved proportionally; trivial or non-material influence need not be inflated into false coauthorship.
- Protection effort should scale with risk, value, ambiguity, circulation and the consequences of losing lineage.

## 5. Two-axis protection model

Credits & Attribution Ops separates two questions that are often collapsed:

1. **Relationship:** how does this material relate to its upstream material?
2. **Custody:** what must remain knowable about this material while it exists, moves and changes?

### 5.1 Relationship classes

Use the narrowest description supported by evidence.

| Relationship | Operational meaning | Required care |
|---|---|---|
| **Original** | Created locally from the available evidence | Do not erase material donor lineage if one exists |
| **Reference / inspiration** | An external work materially informed framing or direction without becoming a direct source artifact | Do not imply copying, endorsement or official derivation |
| **Adaptation** | A source was deliberately reshaped for a new purpose, context or grammar | Identify origin, material changes and local destination |
| **Operational derivation** | A procedure, method or implementation was re-expressed from a donor concept | Separate upstream concept from local implementation and evidence |
| **Generated / AI-mediated derivation** | Identifiable material was supplied to or materially conditioned a generative process | Preserve material input lineage; do not treat model mediation as provenance erasure |
| **Mirror** | The same artifact is transported to another path or surface | Preserve canonical path and fingerprint/version when exact identity matters |
| **Composite** | Several sources materially contribute to one output | Preserve each material lineage and identify the local synthesis |
| **Pending / unclear** | Relationship, authorship or permission scope is unresolved | Keep it provisional and prevent silent canonicalization |

These are operational provenance labels, not legal classifications.

### 5.2 Custody dimensions

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **Asset identity** | What exactly is being protected or tracked? |
| **Origin** | What is the earliest recoverable source or creation point? |
| **Authorship / contribution** | Who created, adapted, maintained or materially contributed which part? |
| **Current custody** | Who or what currently maintains the relevant version or record? |
| **Canonical identity** | Which artifact, path, repository, version or record governs this lineage? |
| **Transformation** | What happened to the material between origin and current form? |
| **Permission envelope** | Which uses are actually known as allowed, restricted, conditional or unresolved? |
| **Disclosure boundary** | What may be public, shared, referenced, mirrored or kept internal? |
| **Derivative lineage** | Which descendants, forks, adaptations or composites came from this material? |
| **Attribution inheritance** | Which origin and transformation facts should accompany descendants? |
| **Evidence** | What supports the claimed identity, date, relationship or transformation? |
| **State** | Is the material active, incubating, disputed, superseded, rejected or archived? |

A project may use only the dimensions its risk profile requires.

## 6. Minimum protection record

Record only what changes the decision, but preserve these fields when relevant:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| **Asset / artifact** | What material is this record about? |
| **Origin** | Where did it come from? |
| **Author / community / contributor** | Who created or materially contributed to it, or is that unknown? |
| **Source lineage** | Is there an upstream, intermediary, fork, mirror, donor or named source artifact? |
| **Exposure / donor context** | Which external architecture, corpus, packet, review or collaboration context was materially available before this artifact or mechanism was produced? |
| **Current custodian** | Who or what maintains the relevant current copy or record? |
| **Canonical identity** | What path, repository, URL, registry entry or source governs identity? |
| **Version / fingerprint** | Which version is meant, and does exact byte identity matter? |
| **Absorbed** | What was actually used? |
| **Transformed** | What changed: extraction, translation, summary, adaptation, remix, generation, redaction, reformatting or other operation? |
| **Rejected / contained** | What was deliberately not imported or was kept bounded? |
| **Relationship** | Original, inspiration, adaptation, derivation, generated derivation, mirror, composite or pending? |
| **Permission status** | What usage basis is actually known? |
| **Permission scope** | Which actions are covered, restricted, conditional or unresolved? |
| **Disclosure boundary** | What may leave the current boundary, and what must remain private or protected? |
| **Destination** | Which source, procedure, registry, mirror, output or archive receives the material? |
| **Derivative links** | Which descendants or local adaptations should remain connected to this lineage? |
| **Attribution inheritance** | What minimum credit or lineage facts should travel with descendants? |
| **Evidence basis** | Which timestamp, history, source record, fingerprint or external evidence supports the record? |
| **Risk** | Is there privacy, authorship, licence, factuality, security, commercial, reputational or disclosure risk? |
| **Canonicalization status** | `analytic_only`, `pending`, `applied`, `mirrored`, `derived`, `disputed` or `rejected`? |
| **State** | Active, incubating, disputed, superseded or archived? |

A protection record can survive rejection or non-use. Rejection must not silently become incorporation later.

## 7. Evidence ladder for content identity

Use only as much identity evidence as the situation deserves.

| Evidence layer | What it can support |
|---|---|
| **Named provenance** | A recoverable statement of source, author or upstream relationship |
| **Explicit donor / adaptation record** | Direct evidence that a source, mechanism or responsibility boundary was intentionally used, adapted or transplanted |
| **Date / version** | Which temporal or released state is being discussed |
| **Canonical path** | Where the governing artifact or record should be retrieved |
| **Revision history** | How the artifact changed over time and who made recorded changes |
| **Chronology + mechanism correspondence** | Evidence that a distinctive mechanism existed upstream before appearing downstream; stronger than resemblance, weaker than an explicit donor record when the transfer channel remains unresolved |
| **Fingerprint / hash** | Exact byte identity for a specific artifact when that matters |
| **Signed or externally witnessed evidence** | Stronger external evidence when the project already has a reason and mechanism to preserve it |

No layer should be described as proving more than it actually proves. A hash can show that two byte sequences match; it cannot establish who authored them or whether their use is lawful.

## 8. Protection operation

Run the smallest version the material requires:

1. **Identify** the asset, source artifact or material contribution actually at issue.
2. **Bound** the operation: decide what is inside the lineage record and what must stay outside it.
3. **Establish lineage** by separating origin, upstream contributors, intermediaries and local material.
4. **Map material exposure** when cross-system or AI-assisted work is involved: record which donor architecture, corpus, packet, review or collaboration context was available before the local mechanism appeared.
5. **Establish custody** by locating the current maintainer, canonical path and relevant version.
6. **Classify transformation**: record what was copied, extracted, translated, summarized, adapted, remixed, generated, redacted, mirrored or rejected.
7. **Resolve the permission envelope only as far as evidence allows**. Do not infer rights from availability, silence or prior access.
8. **Control exposure**: decide what provenance can travel publicly and what private, confidential or security-sensitive detail must remain bounded.
9. **Transport** the material with enough canonical identity to recover its governing source.
10. **Inherit attribution and lineage** into material descendants when the upstream contribution remains material.
11. **Preserve evidence** proportionally through dates, versions, histories, fingerprints or other records when needed.
12. **Audit** the result for silent appropriation, permission drift, broken canonicality, leaked boundaries, lineage drift, attribution totalization and false certainty.

Valid operational outcomes include `no_delta`, `analytic_only`, `protected_reference`, `adapted`, `derived`, `mirrored`, `pending_permission`, `disputed` and `rejected`.

## 9. Transformation and inheritance

Different transformations preserve different kinds of continuity.

| Transformation | Minimum continuity to preserve when material |
|---|---|
| **Copy / mirror** | Canonical origin, version and exact-identity status |
| **Excerpt / extraction** | Source identity and enough context to avoid false independence |
| **Translation** | Original source plus local translator/translation identity |
| **Summary / compression** | Upstream source and local summarization identity when the source materially determines the output |
| **Reformat / port** | Original content lineage plus local transport or implementation changes |
| **Adaptation** | Original source, material changes and local adapter |
| **Fork** | Upstream canonical identity, divergence point and local fork identity |
| **Composite / remix** | Each material lineage plus the local synthesis |
| **AI-mediated generation** | Material input lineage, local operator/project and meaningful transformation notes |
| **Redaction / public-safe projection** | Source lineage internally; public output should reveal only what the disclosure boundary permits |
| **Archive / snapshot** | Historical identity, date/version and an explicit non-current state |

Transformation does not mean every descendant must carry a maximal visible credit block. Attribution can be compact while the deeper lineage record remains recoverable.

## 10. Permission envelope

Credits & Attribution Ops records permission; it does not manufacture or legally resolve it.

When permission matters, track the relevant dimensions separately rather than collapsing them into `yes/no`:

- read / inspect / reference;
- quote / excerpt;
- copy / mirror;
- modify / adapt / translate;
- redistribute / publish;
- commercial reuse;
- model or workflow input, where terms or project policy make that distinction material;
- derivative publication;
- private/internal use;
- other project-specific conditions.

Useful states include `known_allowed`, `known_restricted`, `conditional`, `unknown`, `not_applicable` and `requires_review`.

Do not convert uncertainty into permission. Do not convert attribution into permission.

## 11. Disclosure boundary

A protection record can contain information that should not travel with a public artifact.

Separate:

- **public lineage**: safe origin, authorship, canonical routes and visible credit;
- **bounded operational lineage**: internal transformation or decision detail needed to govern the material;
- **confidential / restricted lineage**: third-party, contractual, personal, security-sensitive or otherwise protected information that must not be exposed merely to make attribution more complete.

A public-facing credit should be sufficient, not invasive. Protecting lineage includes protecting the boundary around the lineage record itself.

## 12. Protection profiles

Use proportional protection rather than maximal bureaucracy.

| Profile | Appropriate when | Typical record |
|---|---|---|
| **Light** | Informal reference, low-risk circulation, easily recoverable source | Source + author/community + local destination or use |
| **Standard** | Publication, adaptation, recurring reuse or meaningful derivative work | Identity + lineage + transformation + permission scope + canonical route + attribution inheritance |
| **High-assurance** | High-value, disputed, commercially sensitive or exact-identity material | Standard record + explicit versions/dates + revision history + derivative links + decision log + fingerprints or stronger external evidence when available |

High-assurance does not mean legal certainty. It means stronger operational traceability.

## 13. Copyable public patterns

### Reference / inspiration

> Informed by **[source]** by **[author/community]**. Local interpretation by **[person/project]**, **[date/version]**. Canonical source: **[path/URL]**.

### Adaptation

> Adapted from **[source]** by **[author/community]**. Local modifications by **[person/project]**, **[date/version]**. Original: **[path/URL]**. Preserve applicable upstream lineage and terms.

### AI-mediated derivation

> Produced through an AI-assisted transformation materially using **[source/material]** by **[author/community]**. Local operation/adaptation by **[person/project]**, **[date/version]**. Canonical upstream source: **[path/URL]**.

### Mirror

> Canonical artifact: **[path/URL]**. This copy is a convenience mirror. Verify version and fingerprint before treating exact byte identity as current.

### Composite

> Materially based on **[sources]**, with local synthesis by **[person/project]**, **[date/version]**. Preserve each material lineage; no endorsement or partnership is implied.

These patterns are public outputs of a deeper custody record. They preserve provenance; they do not grant permission.

## 14. AI-mediated operations

AI systems can increase lineage ambiguity because transformation is cheap and intermediate steps can disappear.

When AI materially participates:

- distinguish the human or project operator from the model and from upstream source authors;
- preserve identifiable material inputs when they materially shape a reusable or published output;
- do not describe model mediation as creating a clean-room origin by default;
- do not infer model authorship, ownership or permission from the fact that a model generated text, code, images or other output;
- record meaningful local selection, editing, synthesis or adaptation separately when it changes authorship or responsibility;
- keep private prompts, confidential corpora or security-sensitive context outside public lineage when disclosure would violate the boundary;
- use uncertainty explicitly when material influence cannot be reconstructed reliably.

The goal is not to log every token. It is to prevent a material lineage from disappearing merely because generation occurred between source and destination.

### 14.1 Provenance transport before canonicalization

When high-resolution architecture, technical design or other materially distinctive source material crosses a project or model boundary, provenance should travel **with the material**, not wait for a later dispute.

A compact **donor envelope** may record:

- source project or artifact;
- author, owner or responsible upstream party as actually known;
- date, revision or canonical locator;
- relationship class: reference, inspiration, adaptation, derivation, composite or unresolved;
- what may be inspected, adapted, transformed or redistributed as actually supported;
- what remains sovereign, confidential or otherwise bounded;
- the minimum attribution or lineage facts expected to survive downstream transformation;
- unresolved provenance questions that must remain unresolved rather than silently becoming local origin.

Before a materially influenced AI-generated or AI-assisted artifact becomes canonical, published or reusable, perform a **provenance readback**:

1. Which named concepts, schemas, responsibility boundaries, sequencing patterns or other distinctive mechanisms may have been materially conditioned by donor context?
2. Which of those were independently present locally before the exposure?
3. Which donor edges are directly documented, which are supported only by chronology/correspondence, and which remain unresolved?
4. Does the proposed local credit preserve both upstream lineage and genuine local authorship?
5. Would removing the donor name from the record make the current artifact falsely appear independently originated?

The target is not maximal credit text. The target is enough recoverable lineage that a later reader can distinguish **local implementation**, **upstream influence**, **direct adaptation**, **composite development** and **uncertainty**.

### 14.2 AI-mediated lineage drift

A recurring AI-native provenance hazard occurs when donor material is available to a model, the model recomposes it into a coherent local artifact, and the transformation is faster or more complete than the provenance record.

Typical sequence:

1. material crosses a trust, review, collaboration or ingestion boundary;
2. the receiving model recomposes useful concepts or mechanisms;
3. local coherence creates a false sense of local origin;
4. an upstream contributor later recognizes distinctive structure;
5. a model or participant may then over-correct by narrating total ancestry, ownership or causal certainty;
6. only a later lineage audit reconstructs the smaller, evidence-backed relationship.

The preventive rule is:

> **AI recomposition does not reset provenance, and local coherence does not prove local origin.**

The corrective rule is equally important:

> **A real donor edge does not imply that the donor owns, authored or parented the whole downstream system.**

Do not use invented percentage ancestry or model-generated contribution shares unless a defined quantitative method and evidence actually support them.

### 14.3 Lineage-conflict audit

When attribution is disputed, begin with the **smallest disputed layer** rather than with motive, character or whole-system ownership.

1. **Specify the object.** Name the exact mechanism, schema, term, file, responsibility boundary, implementation family or artifact under dispute.
2. **Freeze totalizing conclusions.** Do not assume theft, clean-room independence, parenthood, ownership or exclusive origin before the lineage is reconstructed.
3. **Reconstruct chronology.** Compare dated upstream antecedents, recipient-side antecedents, exposure/access windows, implementation dates and later divergence.
4. **Rank evidence.** Prefer explicit donor/adaptation records and mechanism-level correspondence over vocabulary, visual resemblance, development speed or model confidence.
5. **Reverse-audit.** Search for recipient-native antecedents and reciprocal influence with the same seriousness used to search for donor evidence.
6. **Classify narrowly.** Use the smallest supported relationship: independent development, reference/inspiration, adaptation, operational derivation, AI-mediated derivation, composite or unresolved.
7. **Repair the smallest surface.** Correct the affected lineage, attribution, registry, note or canonical source without rewriting unrelated authorship.
8. **Preserve uncertainty.** If the transfer channel cannot be established, record material influence or unresolved provenance without inventing confidential-source causation.

For a claim that a distinctive mechanism came **specifically from confidential or private material**, use a higher threshold. Strong support normally requires all of the following:

- a dated private antecedent;
- no materially equivalent prior public/shared disclosure available to explain the mechanism;
- a plausible access or exposure channel;
- post-exposure appearance of the distinctive mechanism downstream;
- no stronger recipient-native, shared or independent explanation.

Exposure establishes a possible channel. It does not establish derivation by itself.

## 15. Integration contract

When transplanted into another project, this module remains subordinate to that project's own authority, terms and boundaries.

A receiving project should decide locally:

- what counts as an immaterial asset worth protecting;
- where custody and lineage records live;
- which artifacts require visible credits;
- what counts as material influence;
- who can promote a record from pending to applied or canonical;
- what permission authority controls reuse decisions;
- which disclosure classes exist locally;
- which private ledgers must remain private;
- how canonical paths, mirrors, versions and fingerprints are validated;
- when derivative relationships must be recorded;
- when a record is superseded or archived.

Do not import Moon Source-specific identity, links, ownership or public-surface rules as if they governed the receiving project.

## 16. QA and custody audit

Before publishing, mirroring, adapting, generating from or teaching with material, check:

- the protected asset is identifiable;
- origin and material contributors are named where known;
- local authorship or adaptation is distinguished from upstream work;
- external lineage has not been silently absorbed;
- AI mediation has not been used as a provenance reset;
- material donor context was transported or explicitly marked unresolved before canonicalization where appropriate;
- local implementation authorship has not been confused with exclusive lineage;
- a real lineage edge has not been inflated into ownership, parenthood, total derivation or invented percentage ancestry;
- current topology or integration direction has not been substituted for historical lineage;
- transformation history is proportionate and recoverable;
- permission and its scope are no stronger than the evidence;
- public exposure does not cross the material's disclosure boundary;
- no endorsement, partnership, adoption, impact or validation claim was smuggled into the credit;
- canonical source and current destination are recoverable;
- derivative relationships remain connected when material;
- dates, versions and fingerprints are checked when exact identity matters;
- private, confidential or security-sensitive lineage remains outside public credits;
- uncertainty remains explicit where evidence is incomplete.

## 17. Boundary

This module is not:

- the Moon Source license or a substitute for the applicable standard license;
- legal advice or a legal classification system;
- a copyright, trademark, patent or contractual rights resolver;
- an enforcement, takedown or dispute-resolution mechanism;
- proof that a donor authorized a use;
- proof of legal ownership or authorship merely because a record says so;
- proof of external adoption or endorsement;
- a substitute for the receiving project's source authority;
- permission to expose private attribution ledgers or confidential material;
- a requirement to record trivial influence or every intermediate AI operation.

It is an **operational and evidentiary continuity layer for immaterial material**.

## 18. Quick operation

Copy this when a material asset needs protection before or during circulation:

```text
Apply Credits & Attribution Ops to the material below.

Identify:
1. the asset or artifact being protected;
2. recoverable origin and material contributors;
3. current custodian and canonical identity;
4. relationship to upstream material;
5. transformations already performed or planned;
6. permission status and scope only as supported by evidence;
7. public, bounded and confidential disclosure limits;
8. derivative or downstream relationships that should remain connected;
9. minimum attribution that should travel with the material;
10. material donor/exposure context that existed before the current artifact or mechanism;
11. evidence needed for the appropriate protection profile;
12. whether a pre-canonicalization provenance readback or lineage-conflict audit is needed;
13. unresolved risks or uncertainties.

Use the lightest protection profile that preserves what matters.
Do not infer permission, ownership or legal status from availability or attribution.
Do not expose private lineage merely to make a public credit more complete.
```
